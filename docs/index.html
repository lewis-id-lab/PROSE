Genetic augmentation of a clinical risk model for invasive mould disease

A simulation study of incremental value: data report

Published

October 20, 2026

The question

A validated clinical score (Stanzani et al., J Infect 2019) already predicts 60-day invasive mould disease (IMD) in haematological inpatients with an aROC of 0.85. Candidate host variants in PTX3, CLEC7A (dectin-1), CD209 (DC-SIGN), TLR4 and other genes are repeatedly reported as associated with invasive aspergillosis. This project asks whether adding them to the score would change what a clinician does.

It is framed as a value-of-information question rather than a prediction-improvement exercise:

Under what combinations of effect size, risk-allele frequency and linkage structure would host genetic information change clinical decisions for invasive mould disease in haematological patients, and is the published evidence anywhere near those combinations?

A negative answer, that no realistic genetic architecture moves the decision curve, is a finding and not a failure. The primary endpoint is decision-curve net benefit at the 5% threshold, judged against a minimum clinically important difference fixed before any genetic result exists.
