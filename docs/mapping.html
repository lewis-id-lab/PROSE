What “h2” means

PTX3 has three loci in strong linkage (rs2305619, rs3816527, rs1840680). In the phased 1000 Genomes European data the common haplotypes are G-A-G (54%), A-C-A (40%) and A-A-A (6%), with alleles in that locus order.

  • h2 is the chromosome class that carries the A allele at rs2305619: A-C-A, A-A-A and the very rare A-A-G, 45.6% in 1000G EUR and 48.2% in gnomAD NFE. h1 is G-A-G. The model term is an additive per-copy effect of h2 in the recipient.
  • rs1840680 A is the same class (r² = 0.996), so it is an interchangeable tag.
  • rs3816527 C is not interchangeable. It marks only A-C-A, about 87% of h2, so evidence reported on it is mapped to h2 through the haplotype structure and used as a cross-check.

How each study is converted

Studies report PTX3 in different ways: dominant or recessive contrasts, allelic odds ratios, or genotype counts, with the contrast sometimes written in both directions. Each one is converted to a single scale, the log odds ratio per h2 copy, by finding the effect that a haplotype model would need to produce the study’s contrast. The model is additive on the log-odds scale, uses Hardy-Weinberg haplotype pairs and case-control sampling, and the standard error follows by the delta method. The direction of each contrast is read from the comparison text, including which side is the reference.

The primary estimate is an odds ratio of 1.35 per h2 copy, with a 90% interval of 0.99 to 1.81. The interval just reaches 1, and the prediction interval for a new study is wide (an odds ratio of 0.81 to 2.21), so the evidence is weak. Herrero-Sanchez 2018 first appeared to point the other way; reading the paper showed that the extraction followed a reference label that is reversed in the source, and corrected it is positive (see the next section). Adding it raises the estimate and tightens the interval, and dropping Brunel 2018 lowers the estimate and widens it.

Does the h2 definition hold?

If h2 explains the signal, mapping a study through rs3816527 instead of the h2 tag SNP should give about the same per-copy effect.

Code 
cross |>
  transmute(Study = study_id, `Through the h2 tag SNP` = beta_tag, `Through rs3816527` = beta_rs3816527, Difference = difference) |>
  gt() |>
  fmt_number(2:4, decimals = 2)

The two agree to within about 0.15 for every study, which supports defining h2 by the rs2305619 and rs1840680 A alleles.

CLEC7A: the Y238X haplotype

Y238X (the C allele of rs16910526 on the forward strand) sits only on the C-G haplotype of rs16910526 and rs7309123, which is 7.7% of chromosomes in gnomAD NFE (A-G is 34.9% and A-C 57.4%). The term is the per-copy effect of that haplotype. Studies that write the allele as G are on the opposite strand, and are complemented.

Among the small odds-ratio studies Y238X is null. The largest study (Fisher 2017, 483 cases) reports hazard ratios above 1, and including it moves the pooled effect up. The second CLEC7A effect is the per-copy effect of the A-G haplotype, the G allele of rs7309123 on the non-Y238X background (A-C is the reference). rs7309123 is a C/G palindrome, so the strand each study used had to be determined from its control frequencies and the papers themselves; the evidence for each study is in data/rs7309123_strand_resolution.csv and in addendum 16 of the decision record. Studies are graded A to D, and only grades A and B (resolved with at least low-to-medium confidence; the full texts of Kalkanci 2020 and Chen 2019 settled their strands, addendum 19) enter the primary estimate.

The primary estimate rests on three studies (Schnetzke 2024, Kalkanci 2020 and Sainz 2012) and is an odds ratio of 1.86 per A-G copy, with a 90% interval from 1.26 to 2.63. The interval excludes 1 in every analysis. Adding the Jena cohort, whose strand is only weakly resolved, gives 1.92, and taking every study as forward strand gives 1.54. The row that adds the non-European studies is not a valid estimate (their contrasts are mapped through European haplotype frequencies) and is shown only to document why they are excluded.

TLR4: the G-T haplotype

The term is the G-T haplotype of rs4986790 (G) and rs4986791 (T), 6.1% of chromosomes in gnomAD NFE. No cohort has minor homozygotes, so every study enters as carriers against non-carriers, with 0.5 added to a zero cell (decision D3c).

            SE = se_beta, `OR per copy` = or_per_copy) |>
  gt() |>
  fmt_number(c(`Log OR as reported`, `Per-copy effect`, SE, `OR per copy`), decimals = 2)
Code 
tlr4$pooled |>
  transmute(Analysis = scenario, Studies = k, OR = or, `OR 5%` = or_lo, `OR 95%` = or_hi, tau = tau) |>
  gt() |>
  fmt_number(c(OR, `OR 5%`, `OR 95%`, tau), decimals = 2)

The effect is positive but uncertain. The two SNPs disagree within a large sampling error, which is expected from carrier counts this small. de Boer 2011 analysed donors and recipients and the extraction does not say which genotype its rows use, so it is left out of the primary analysis; treating it as a recipient study raises the estimate. Tanpaibule’s allele is carried by none of its 33 cases against 11 of 295 controls, so its estimate rests on the continuity correction.

CD209: G at rs4804800

The two CD209 SNPs (rs4804800 and rs7248637, r² = 0.41) give four haplotypes in gnomAD NFE: G-A 7.8%, A-A 2.6%, G-G 4.7% and A-G 84.9%. The term is the per-copy effect of the chromosome class that carries G at rs4804800 (G-A and G-G, 12.5%). rs4804800 rows map to it directly. rs7248637 rows (the A allele) map through the haplotype structure and are used where a study has no rs4804800 row. A second CD209 parameter is not estimated.

cd209$pooled |>

  gt() |>

The effect is positive in all three studies. The two studies that also report rs7248637 agree with their rs4804800 effect (differences of 0.21 and 0.07 on the log scale), which supports mapping the rs7248637 evidence to the same class. Adding Fisher 2017 (hazard ratios, rs7248637) lowers the pooled odds ratio to 1.45, with an interval that includes 1. Sainz 2011’s two rows cannot be mapped: an allele is missing and the comparison has more than two groups.

IL10 and IFNG: dropped from the panel

IL10 rs1800896 and IFNG rs2069705 are single SNPs. The risk allele is the forward-strand allele that most studies report as their effect allele (IL10 C, reported as G; IFNG G, reported as C), and a study that reports the other allele is mapped to the same class with the sign reversed. Both were mapped as optional terms, then dropped from the panel on 19 October 2026 (addendum 10) because the evidence is too thin to estimate an effect.

Code 
bind_rows(
  il10$study |> mutate(Term = "IL10 rs1800896 C", Study = study_label(il10$study)),
  ifng$study |> mutate(Term = "IFNG rs2069705 G", Study = study_label(ifng$study))
) |>
  transmute(Term, Study, `Evidence used` = paste(tier, model, measure, sep = ", "), `Per-copy effect` = beta, SE = se_beta, `OR per copy` = or_per_copy) |>
  gt() |>
  fmt_number(c(`Per-copy effect`, SE, `OR per copy`), decimals = 2)
Code 
bind_rows(il10$pooled |> mutate(Term = "IL10"), ifng$pooled |> mutate(Term = "IFNG")) |>
  transmute(Term, Analysis = scenario, Studies = k, OR = or, `OR 5%` = or_lo, `OR 95%` = or_hi, tau = tau) |>
  gt() |>
  fmt_number(c(OR, `OR 5%`, `OR 95%`, tau), decimals = 2)

IL10 rests on one study (Tanpaibule 2020, a dominant odds ratio of about 5), so its pooled value is that study shrunk by the prior; adding de Boer 2011, whose genotype source is unstated, gives two studies. The two IFNG studies disagree in direction, and the hazard-ratio fallback adds nothing because Fisher’s IFNG rows are donor genotype only. Putting either term back would be a sensitivity analysis.

Limits

  • The rows are unverified except where stated above, and the checks so far are the assistant’s, not the human round-2 check.
  • Brunel 2018 states “the minor allele”, so its effect allele is inferred from European frequencies, and the frequency check above casts doubt on it.
  • The Chinese and Thai cohorts use European haplotype frequencies.
  • Three to five studies per term is few. The priors on the pooled effect and on tau are proposals.
  • Open questions from addendum 8 that the human checks did not settle: whether to use Herrero-Sanchez’s invasive aspergillosis rows and whether to keep Brunel in the primary analysis. The rs7309123 strand is resolved with some confidence for six of the ten studies and weakly for three more; only Zhao 2022 is undetermined (addenda 16 and 19). Hazard-ratio studies join a term’s primary analysis only when it has fewer than three odds-ratio studies (addendum 9).

## CD209: G at rs4804800

## IL10 and IFNG: dropped from the panel

IL10 rs1800896 and IFNG rs2069705 are single SNPs. The risk allele is the forward-strand allele that most studies report as their effect
allele (IL10 C, reported as G; IFNG G, reported as C), and a study that reports the other allele is mapped to the same class with the sign
reversed. Both were mapped as optional terms, then **dropped from the panel on 19 October 2026 (addendum 10)** because the evidence is too thin
to estimate an effect.

```{r}
#| label: tbl-optional
#| tbl-cap: "IL10 and IFNG: odds-ratio evidence has fewer than three studies, so hazard-ratio studies were added (the fallback rule); the pooled value is mostly the prior."
bind_rows(
  il10$study |> mutate(Term = "IL10 rs1800896 C", Study = study_label(il10$study)),
  ifng$study |> mutate(Term = "IFNG rs2069705 G", Study = study_label(ifng$study))
) |>
  transmute(Term, Study, `Evidence used` = paste(tier, model, measure, sep = ", "), `Per-copy effect` = beta, SE = se_beta, `OR per copy` = or_per_copy) |>
  gt() |>
  fmt_number(c(`Per-copy effect`, SE, `OR per copy`), decimals = 2)
```

```{r}
#| label: tbl-optional-pooled
#| tbl-cap: "IL10 and IFNG pooled effect, first row of each term is the primary (fallback) analysis."
bind_rows(il10$pooled |> mutate(Term = "IL10"), ifng$pooled |> mutate(Term = "IFNG")) |>
  transmute(Term, Analysis = scenario, Studies = k, OR = or, `OR 5%` = or_lo, `OR 95%` = or_hi, tau = tau) |>
  gt() |>
  fmt_number(c(OR, `OR 5%`, `OR 95%`, tau), decimals = 2)
```

IL10 rests on one study (Tanpaibule 2020, a dominant odds ratio of about 5), so its pooled value is that study shrunk by the prior; adding
de Boer 2011, whose genotype source is unstated, gives two studies. The two IFNG studies disagree in direction, and the hazard-ratio
fallback adds nothing because Fisher's IFNG rows are donor genotype only. Putting either term back would be a sensitivity analysis.

## Limits

- The rows are unverified except where stated above, and the checks so far are the assistant's, not the human round-2 check.
- Brunel 2018 states "the minor allele", so its effect allele is inferred from European frequencies, and the frequency check above
  casts doubt on it.
- The Chinese and Thai cohorts use European haplotype frequencies.
- Three to five studies per term is few. The priors on the pooled effect and on tau are proposals.
- Open questions from addendum 8 that the human checks did not settle: whether to use Herrero-Sanchez's invasive aspergillosis rows
  and whether to keep Brunel in the primary analysis. The rs7309123 strand is resolved with some confidence for six of the ten studies and weakly for three more; only Zhao 2022 is undetermined (addenda 16 and 19). Hazard-ratio studies join a term's primary
  analysis only when it has fewer than three odds-ratio studies (addendum 9).
