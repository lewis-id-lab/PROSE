2. Search and screening

What Job A fixed, and what it did not

PTX3 is transformed. rs2305619 went from 15 poolable rows and 2 with genotype counts to 45 and 32; rs3816527 from 20 and 8 to 39 and 31; rs1840680 from 6 and 1 to 36 and 30. Most of that comes from Tang 2020, whose full genotype tables sat beyond the old character cap.

TLR4 is not fixed and cannot be fixed by extraction. rs4986790 now has 9 studies and 2 poolable rows; rs4986791 has 9 studies and 1. Fisher 2017 analysed both but published no estimate or counts for either — they are in its supplemental Table 1, which is not in the supplied PDF. Fischer 2016 did not genotype them. Konuma 2026 notes both are close to monomorphic in Asian populations. The binding constraint is the missing supplementary files, not the extraction, so TLR4 needs supplementary retrieval or author contact before the haplotype block can be parameterised.

Four studies have supplementary tables that are absent from the supplied PDFs and hold genotype counts or null estimates: S049 (Suppl. Tables III and IV), S080 (supplemental Table 1), S027 (Table S1), S071 (Table S1).

131 rows were added, replacing 102. The table now holds 1449 variant rows, of which 757 are meta-analysable — meaning they carry either a complete effect estimate with an orientable effect allele, or a complete six-cell genotype table with both alleles named.

The important finding is that the TLR4 problem was partly a question of looking for the wrong field. Three studies (Ceesay 2016, Kalkanci 2020, Grube 2013) print complete 2x3 genotype tables for both TLR4 SNPs and no odds ratio at all — they report only a chi-squared or Fisher p. Two more (Aki 2015, Tanpaibule 2020) add partial or complete counts. An odds ratio under any genetic model can be computed from those counts, so these are not author-contact targets.

The structural catch is that every one of these cohorts has zero minor homozygotes in both cases and controls for rs4986790 and rs4986791 — for example Grube 2013 rs4986790 is 0/6/35 in cases and 0/7/100 in controls. Only a dominant or carrier model is estimable, and a continuity correction will be needed. Tanpaibule 2020’s rs4986790 is degenerate in the other direction (cases 33/0/0), which is why the paper prints a dash rather than an estimate.

Two expectations were wrong and are corrected here.

Fisher 2017’s supplemental Table 1 is genotyping and imputation quality control, not effect estimates — alleles, minor allele frequency, call rate, Hardy-Weinberg p and pass/fail across three cohorts for 25 SNPs. It does not advance TLR4 on the estimate route. What it does deliver is 25 nucleotide allele pairs with per-cohort frequencies, which makes it the review’s single largest allele source, since many SNPs carry effect estimates elsewhere in the table with no alleles attached. Two cautions: the Alleles column carries no footnote defining major/minor order, and it disagrees with Schnetzke 2024’s explicitly major/minor listing for rs3816527 (C/A against A/C), so the order cannot be assumed. Five SNPs failed QC in Cohort 1 only — rs4833095, rs767455, rs3901533, rs4804800, rs7252229.

Schnetzke 2024’s Table S2 gives three-genotype distributions for the stratification (n=186) and validation (n=138) cohorts but does not split them by invasive fungal disease status, so no 2x2 can be reconstructed and the per-genotype case and control counts remain unavailable. genotype_counts_available is corrected to no for that study. rs1840680 does not appear in Table S2 at all, only in a footnote stating complete linkage disequilibrium with rs2305619.

Brunel 2018 delivered what was expected, and it bears directly on Aim 3b. Supplementary Figure 2 reports rs2305619 and rs3816527 separately, which the main paper did not, in three strata: all episodes, no neutropenia at presentation, and neutropenia at presentation. The non-neutropenic result that the combined hazard ratio of 5.06 came from decomposes into rs2305619 homozygous 8/33 versus other 7/100 (P=0.008) and rs3816527 homozygous 9/37 versus other 5/92 (P=0.002), against P=0.7 and P=0.6 in the neutropenic stratum. No per-SNP hazard ratio is printed anywhere — all three Cox models in Supplementary Table 1 use the combined “and/or” variable.

Herrero-Sanchez 2018 carries a scope caveat recorded on every row: none of its supplementary tables uses proven or probable invasive mould disease as the endpoint. They use Candida and/or Aspergillus detection, fungal colonisation, or Candida colonisation. Those rows may not be eligible for the primary synthesis.

6. Baseline model — verified, with one gap

Everything needed is in the Stanzani paper and has been re-read from it: the equation and intercept, each predictor’s prevalence separately in cases and non-cases, and the validation targets.

The joint distribution of the seven predictors is not published, and it is load-bearing. Sampling 200,000 admissions independently from the published marginals and applying the fixed equation gives an IMD prevalence of 2.27% against 3.3%, and an aROC of 0.775 against 0.85. High-risk chemotherapy and prolonged neutropenia are close to the same clinical state and cannot be drawn independently.

A Gaussian copula with a single exchangeable latent correlation gives:

latent rho simulated prevalence simulated aROC
0.00 2.27% 0.769
0.20 2.92% 0.829
0.30 3.32% 0.852
0.40 3.75% 0.872
published 3.3% 0.85

rho = 0.30 reproduces both global targets almost exactly but misses the per-predictor case-group prevalences — uncontrolled malignancy 74% simulated against 59% published, corticosteroids 38% against 23%, high-risk chemotherapy 54% against 64%. One exchangeable parameter spreads correlation evenly across pairs that are clinically very differently related, so a structured matrix fitted to the 16 published targets is needed, with clinical constraints to make it identifiable.

Worth requesting from Bologna: 2x2 cross-tabulations for chemotherapy x neutropenia, neutropenia x lymphopenia and malignancy status x chemotherapy, plus the distribution of admissions per patient. That would convert an assumption into data.

Update, 17 October 2026 — correlation structure fitted. A structured latent correlation matrix (non-negative dependence, clinically grouped) was fitted to the 16 published targets. The first version had eight parameters and reduced chi-square against the targets from 346 under independence and 47 under the exchangeable rho = 0.30 to 8.6. Two problems remained: uncontrolled malignancy in cases was simulated at 69% against 59% published (2.3 standard errors), and two parameters sat at their upper bound because the chemotherapy-lymphopenia correlation was tied to the block baseline, which also set malignancy’s links. Version 2 adds a separate chemotherapy-lymphopenia parameter (nine in all) and lets the block extras reach 0.7. Chi-square falls to 0.36, the largest miss is 0.4 standard errors, and the malignancy residual is gone. The fitted matrix has malignancy almost uncorrelated with the rest of the block (about 0.04) and chemotherapy, neutropenia and lymphopenia at 0.69 to 0.74 with each other. d_chemo_neut sits at its 0.7 cap, but the loss is lowest there, so the cap is not costing fit. Positive definiteness is no longer limiting (smallest eigenvalue 0.26).

Read the near-zero chi-square with care. The 16 targets collapse to about nine independent constraints, since the non-case prevalences follow from the case prevalences and the overall prevalence, and the model now has nine parameters. A saturated fit is expected to be this good and does not validate the structure. A 150-matrix ensemble (six chains, R-hat at most 1.09) shows the parameters are wide: the chemotherapy-neutropenia extra 0.24 to 0.68, steroids-CMV 0.02 to 0.60, the malignancy-chemotherapy extra 0.01 to 0.42 (5th to 95th percentiles). The Bologna cross-tabulations, especially malignancy by chemotherapy, would turn these into data.

Decision curves for thresholds of 1 to 30% are now exact functions of the profile distribution, which comes from numerical integration of the fitted copula (no patient-level sampling). On the fitted cohort the minimum clinically important difference is +0.00428 net benefit at the 5% threshold (ensemble 90% range 0.00397 to 0.00460) and +0.00140 at 20% (0.00117 to 0.00167). Baseline net benefit at 5% is 0.01478, with 21.4 true and 125.7 false positives per 1,000 admissions and 14.7% flagged. The curves are step functions because there are only 128 clinical profiles. See scripts/01_fit_correlation.R, 02_correlation_ensemble.R and 03_decision_curves.R, and the baseline and decision-curve pages of the report website. The first-version outputs are kept in output/v1/.

6b. Phase 3 gate — settled 16 October 2026

Recipient genotype is the primary exposure, with donor genotype as a pre-specified secondary restricted to the allogeneic HSCT stratum. The panel is four haplotype terms — PTX3 (rs2305619/rs3816527/rs1840680), CLEC7A (rs16910526/rs7309123, 3 haplotypes), TLR4 (rs4986790/rs4986791) and CD209 (rs4804800/rs7248637, 4 haplotypes) — with IL10 rs1800896 and IFNG rs2069705 optional. The minimum clinically important difference is 20% more true cases of invasive mould disease at no increase in false positives. It was stated as +0.00420 net benefit at the 5% threshold and +0.00191 at 20% on an indicative cohort, and was re-derived on the calibrated cohort on 17 October 2026 as +0.00428 at 5% (primary) and +0.00140 at 20% (secondary). Full record in phase3_decisions.md. All sub-decisions are now settled (17 October 2026): 5% is the primary MCID threshold; TLR9 rs352140 is dropped (D3a); Herrero-Sanchez 2018 is excluded from the primary analysis and run as a sensitivity analysis (D3b); TLR4 is pooled under a carrier model with a continuity correction (D3c); the genetic term is recentred to hold prevalence (D5); the baseline aROC target is 0.85 (D6); and the haplotype phase is coupling, confirmed arithmetically (E1).

The CLEC7A and CD209 terms were collapsed from single SNPs to haplotypes after a linkage check: rs16910526 x rs7309123 sit at D’ = 1.000 with the Y238X allele entirely on one rs7309123 background, and rs4804800 x rs7248637 at r2 = 0.413, D’ = 0.671 with all four haplotypes present. Entering either pair as independent additive terms would repeat the predecessor thesis’s error within a different gene.

7. What blocks the genetic layer

  1. Round-2 verification of the extraction — not started.
  2. Allele orientation: 188 rows across the whole table are reported on the opposite strand from the European segregating pair, and 130 are palindromic and unresolvable from alleles alone. For the panel this reduces to 10 strand-flip cells and the 7 rs7309123 cells — see panel_verification_worklist.csv.
  3. Cohort-overlap adjudication: the Spanish registry series (Sainz / Lupiañez / Sánchez-Maldonado, ten records 2007–2020) and the Jena AML cohorts (S018 n=186, S023 n=164) are not independent studies.
  4. Final panel derivation from panel_candidates.csv — 13 variants now have three or more meta-analysable studies. Four provisional-panel entries — rs5743836 (TLR9), rs1800450 (MBL2), rs1800795 (IL6), rs1800972 (DEFB1) — have no coverage in the included set; CLEC1A rs2306894 has one study.
  5. Allele frequencies: 7 entries in candidate_snp_seed_table.csv still PENDING against gnomAD v4 NFE.
  6. Haplotype frequencies for the PTX3 and TLR4 blocks have not been derived. Derived 17 October 2026 for all four blocks directly from phased 1000 Genomes European genotypes (503 samples, 1,006 chromosomes) and rescaled to gnomAD NFE margins by iterative proportional fitting: data/haplotype_frequencies_phased.csv, data/haplotype_ld_phased.csv, scripts/04_haplotype_frequencies.R. The phase is coupling in every pair, confirmed from the phased chromosomes. The PTX3 trio has three common haplotypes (G-A-G 54%, A-C-A 40%, A-A-A 6% in 1000G EUR), not two; see the addendum to phase3_decisions.md.
  7. TLR4 supplementary retrieval or author contact — Fisher 2017’s supplemental Table 1 and three other supplementary files hold the estimates and counts that would make the TLR4 block poolable.

8. Suggested order of work

  1. Code the baseline equation and its verification harness — all inputs confirmed, no dependencies.
  2. Fit the correlation structure and report reproduction against all 16 published targets.
  3. Build the decision-curve machinery on the baseline alone, so net benefit at 5% and 20% is working before any genetic term exists.
  4. In parallel, run round-2 verification starting with the 13 provenance records, then the 310 P1 rows of 1380 flagged in total, then the six panel variants with four or more independent cohorts (rs16910526, rs2305619, rs7309123, rs3816527, rs4986790, rs4986791), verified line by line against source tables rather than sampled.
  5. Derive the panel, then the frequencies and haplotypes, then the genetic layer.
